# Tone of Voice Framework

Your tone is how your brand sounds. It should feel like one person talking, even when the language changes.

## 1. Pick 3 words

Choose three words that describe how your brand sounds.

Example: warm, direct, confident.

For each word, also write what it is NOT.

Example: warm, but not cheesy. Direct, but not rude.

## 2. Say it like a person

Write how you'd explain your brand to a friend over chai or coffee. Keep it casual. This is your natural voice, and the rest of your writing should sound close to it.

## 3. Words you use and words you avoid

| We say | We never say |
|---|---|
| (everyday words you like) | (jargon, filler, corporate talk) |

## 4. Tone across languages

The same brand can sound different in each language while still feeling like one brand.

| Language | How it sounds | Things to watch |
|---|---|---|
| English | | |
| Arabic | | Formal vs casual Arabic, dialect vs Modern Standard |
| Hindi | | Hindi vs Hinglish, formal vs everyday words |

Questions to answer:

- Which language do you use for which kind of message?
- Do you mix languages in one post, or keep them separate?
- Which words do you keep in their original language because translating them loses the feeling?

## 5. Same message, three ways

Write one message (a welcome, an announcement, a thank you) in each language. Read them side by side. Do they feel like the same brand?

## 6. Before you post

- Does it sound like us?
- Would our customer understand it on the first read?
- Did someone who speaks the language check it, not just a translator?
